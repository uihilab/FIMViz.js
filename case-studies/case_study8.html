Case Study 8 — The Oroville storms, replayed warmer

California DWR's gridded weather generator read as NetCDF4 in the browser: the same January and February 2017 daily weather under six climate scenarios, split into rain and snow per cell and day, and summarized over the Feather River basin above Oroville. Protocol: CASE_STUDY_PROTOCOL.md §0 and §2.

The question. Storms in January and February 2017 filled Lake Oroville and led to the Oroville Dam spillway emergency. How much of that precipitation over the Feather River basin fell as rain, and how much of the snow would have fallen as rain if the same weather had been 1 to 5 °C warmer?

A day's precipitation counts as rain in a cell when that day's mean temperature there is above the threshold, and as snow otherwise. Per day and cell: pr.combine([mask], { op: "min" }) keeps the precipitation where the mask is open, pr.difference(rain) is the snow, and a combine(days, { op: "sum" }) totals the window. The rule is one daily threshold per 6 km cell; it does not model snow within a day or across elevations inside a cell.
